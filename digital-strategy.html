Digital Strategy

Your website, search, Pinterest and email are already one customer journey.

Digital strategy is the plain-English map of how those pieces pass a person along today, where they get stuck or drop off, and what to fix first. It is not more jargon on top of what you already have. It is the thing that tells you which piece to work on next.

In plain English

Five channels, one job each, and one job connecting them.

You probably already have most of these pieces. Strategy is just making sure each one hands the person to the next instead of leaving them stranded.

  1. 01

    Website

    The destination. Where someone finally decides yes or no, so it has to load fast, explain what you do, and give a clear next step.

  2. 02

    Search

    How Google, Bing and AI answers find and describe you. If your pages do not answer the actual question someone typed, they never get this far.

  3. 03

    Pinterest

    Discovery for people still planning what to buy, make or book. A pin’s only job is to send the right person to the right page.

  4. 04

    Email

    The follow-up. It brings back the person who almost said yes, and it is usually the cheapest leak to fix.

  5. 05

    Tools

    Booking links, quote forms, checkout, your CRM. The small connections between channels that quietly break more often than the channels themselves.

The four shapes

Every business fits one of these journeys, or a mix of two.

The map looks different for a shop than it does for a coach, but the idea is the same: find where the path is clear, and where it breaks.

Product business

From discovery to a repeat order

  1. Discovery on Pinterest or search
  2. Lands on the right product page
  3. Cart, with nothing slowing checkout down
  4. Purchase completes without friction
  5. Retention: email brings them back

Shops, makers, handmade brands, boutiques and digital products.

Service business

From discovery to a booked client

  1. Discovery through local search, Maps or referral
  2. Lands on the right service page
  3. Trust: reviews and photos of real work
  4. Inquiry: a call, quote request or message
  5. Booking confirms the appointment or project

Salons, spas, contractors, home services, coaches and consultants.

Local business

From a nearby search to a visit

  1. Search or Maps for a “near me” term
  2. Reviews and photos decide who gets picked
  3. A call or a visit, often the same day

Restaurants, retail storefronts, studios and any business people drive to.

Course or coach

From content to a paying client

  1. Content: a blog post, pin or video that helps first
  2. Lead magnet trades a next step for an email
  3. Email nurtures with more useful content
  4. Offer: the course, program or coaching call

Coaches, course creators and anyone selling knowledge or guidance.

Where people usually leak

Not the whole system. Almost always one weak link.

A pin sending traffic to the homepage instead of the product. A service page with no way to book. An email list that never hears from you again after the first message. The roadmap finds the leak that is actually costing you customers, so you fix that first instead of guessing.

What does digital strategy actually mean?

It means looking at your website, search visibility, Pinterest, email and any tools you use as one connected path instead of separate projects, then finding the exact spot where people are dropping off before they buy, book or reach out.

Do I need a full rebuild to fix my digital strategy?

Usually not. Most businesses have one or two real leaks, not a broken system. A roadmap tells you which piece to fix first, and often that is a page, an email sequence or a missing link, not a full rebuild.

How is this different from an SEO or Pinterest audit?

An SEO or Pinterest audit looks closely at one channel. A digital strategy roadmap looks at how all of your channels connect and hand a visitor off to the next step, so you can see the whole path a customer takes, not just one piece of it.

What do I get at the end?
