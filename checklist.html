Free Pinterest Checklist

5 Pinterest SEO mistakes costing you traffic.

If your pins aren’t getting clicks, it’s usually not one big thing: it’s a handful of small gaps quietly keeping Pinterest from showing your content to the right people. Here are the five I see most often. Check yourself against each one.

Check yourself

The five gaps I see most often.

  1. 01

    Your profile doesn’t actually say what you sell

    Pinterest is a search engine before it’s a social platform. If your bio is vague or brand-voice-only, Pinterest doesn’t know what to rank you for, and neither does a stranger scrolling past. Fix: your bio should say, in plain words, who you help and what you offer.

  2. 02

    Your board titles are cute, not searchable

    “Sparkle & Shine” sounds nice. It ranks for nothing. A clever board name with no keyword in it is a missed opportunity every time someone searches. Fix: rename boards using the actual terms your buyer types into the search bar.

  3. 03

    Your pin titles and descriptions don’t match how people search

    There’s a difference between how you talk about your product and how someone searches for it. Fix: lead with the keyword, then the benefit. Save the personality-driven language for the second half of the description.

  4. 04

    You’re pinning without checking what’s already working

    Posting consistently feels productive, but if you’re not looking at which pins are driving saves and clicks, you’re guessing every time. Fix: check your top 5 performing pins once a month, and do more of whatever they have in common.

  5. 05

    Your website link doesn’t match the promise of the pin

    Someone clicks a pin because it promised them something specific. If the landing page doesn’t deliver that exact thing fast, they bounce. Fix: every pin should link to the specific page that matches its exact promise, not your homepage.
