Pinterest finds your account before it finds your pins.

Pinterest works more like a search engine than a social feed. When someone searches, it looks at your name, your bio, your boards, and the pages your pins link to, then decides whether you belong in the results.

If you opened an account, made pins for a few weeks, and heard nothing back, the account may never have told Pinterest who you are. That part is fixable, and it comes before the pins.

The details

The numbers most people guess at.

  • 100 and 40. A pin title can run 100 characters, and only the first 40 or so show in the feed.
  • 800. A description can run 800 characters. It doesn’t show in the feed, but Pinterest reads it.
  • 2:4.2. Pins taller than this get cut off.
  • Four ways to claim your website. You can delete the tag or file afterward.
  • The Etsy catch. Pinterest says most stores hosted on marketplaces like Etsy can’t be claimed, even though some guides tell you to do it. If your shop lives there, step four works differently, and the checklist shows how.
